1.2Why no AI agent does this

1.3The one rule the domain turns on — PS-8

PS-8

D-06 computes the risk-based size; it does not propose it. The output is "at a 1 % risk budget and a stop at 410 this is 13 shares" — a consequence of stated assumptions, not an answer to "how much should I buy".

The reason is a collision you will meet in Part 3: risk-based sizing gives high-volatility names small positions, and your mandate (M-2) says concentration in the high-volatility AI/semiconductor theme is a deliberate bet. PART 2

The one idea: risk is what you lose at the stop, not what you deploy

The single most confused idea in position sizing, stated before anything is built on it.

2.1The arithmetic

risk_capital = capital × r   ·   d = entry − stop   ·   shares = risk_capital / d   ·   notional = shares × entry PS-2: the basis is max-loss-at-stop. Not conviction, not rank score, not fair value — the one quantity a stop makes knowable in advance. A candidate with no stop gets no size (PS-5): sizing without a stop is not conservative, it is undefined.

Notice what connects risk to deployment: notional / risk_capital = entry / d. The tightness of the stop is the multiplier, and nothing else is.

2.2Same risk, wildly different positions

From L1-portfolio-strategy §5.1a, asserted in test_the_same_risk_budget_gives_wildly_different_positions (risk 2,000, entry 100):

Consequence

"I risk 1 % per trade" says nothing about how concentrated the book is. A tight stop on a volatile name is a huge position with a small risk budget — and it will be stopped out often. That is why the stop should come from volatility (Part 3): an eyeballed stop is an unstated position-size decision.

2.3Case study — DELL in TFSA_2

Take the real TFSA_2 balance (76,805.55 CAD, reported by BMO as of 2026-10-25), a placeholder ceiling of 1 %, and a stated entry 450 USD / stop 410 USD. The footer rate in the same export is 1 USD = 1.3999 CAD (RC-72: the current rate, not the per-line purchase rate).

risk_capital  = 76,805.55 × 0.01                 =    768.06 CAD

shares (risk) = 768.06 / 56.00                  =     13.72
notional      = 13.72 × 450                     =  6,172.32 USD   (8,640 CAD, 11.2 % of the account)

Computed by size_trade itself, not by hand. Now the twist: TFSA_2 holds only 105.24 CAD of cash (75.18 USD at the footer rate). So the real answer is 0 shares — cash binds (Part 5). That is the correct answer about your account on that date, and the tool says so instead of returning 13.

PART 3

Where the stop comes from: volatility

A stop picked by eye is an unstated volatility assumption — and, through entry/d, an unstated size.

3.1The volatility stop — PS-6

d = k · σ̂ · √h · entry   ⇒   shares = risk_capital / (k · σ̂ · √h · entry)   ⇒   shares ∝ 1/σ̂ σ̂ = daily volatility (sd of daily log returns) · h = 21 sessions, the one-month holding horizon (HorizonSet v1) · k = how many horizon standard deviations below entry the stop sits. Code: src/portfolio/sizing/stop_distance.py, MethodEntry stop_distance_vol_v1.

The √h is the square-root-of-time rule: if daily returns are independent with the same variance, the variance of an h-day return is h·σ². It is an assumption, and a weak one in a crisis, when returns cluster (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Labys, 2003, on realised volatility and its persistence).

3.2What σ the tool uses — realised, labelled (PS-23)

D-04 publishes no volatility forecast, so D-06 uses realised volatility — the sample sd of the last 21 daily log returns — and labels it realised, never "forecast" (PS-23, realised_sigma). The difference matters: realised vol is backward-looking and understates exactly when it matters, before an earnings gap. Fewer than 22 closes gives unavailable, never a shorter window (AC-F14.8).

Measured 2026-10-25

DELL, FMP raw closes, 21 sessions to 2026-10-23: σ̂ = 0.0424 per day (≈ 67 % annualised). With k = 1.5: d = 1.5 × 0.0424 × √21 × 450 = 131.09 USD — a stop at 318.91, 29 % below entry. At the same 768 CAD of risk that is 4 shares (1,883 USD), not 13. Your stated stop at 410 implies k = 0.46 — less than half a horizon standard deviation (PS-7 reports this on every answer).

3.3Why k matters: how often a stop is hit

If the log price is a driftless random walk, the reflection principle gives the probability that it touches −k·σ·√h at some point within h days:

P(stop hit within h) = 2 · (1 − Φ(k))

3.4The collision with your mandate — §5.3

shares ∝ 1/σ

High-volatility names get small positions. Your AI/semiconductor theme is high-volatility, and M-2 says concentration in it is deliberate. A textbook vol-scaled rule (Moreira & Muir, 2017, show volatility-managed portfolios improve Sharpe ratios across factors) is a diversifying pressure — the low-volatility tilt QA-45 warns about, arriving in a second domain. This is why the answer is a consequence (PS-8), and why your own size is never capped (PS-10).

3.5Why a CAD listing or CDR gets no σ

RC-53 (measured 2026-08-25): asking FMP for the bare ticker BMO returns the NYSE listing in USD — the right company, wrong venue and wrong currency, and a plausible number. So the tool never fetches σ for a CAD entry by its ticker (AC-F14.8). For a CDR you ruled out substituting the US underlying's σ (F14 D11): a CDR is a separate stock (RC-58). Give --sigma and it is labelled stated; otherwise implied k is unavailable and a volatility stop is refused (PS-24).

PART 4

The account risk budget: allowance, committed, remaining, slots

One trade's risk is bounded by a ceiling. The account's total is bounded too — and those are two different numbers.

4.1Per account, never netted — QS-3, PS-30

You ruled (2026-08-18, QS-3) that the budget is per account: TFSA_1, TFSA_2, RRSP_1, RESP_1 hold capital that cannot be moved between them, so a fund-level budget would abstract over something you cannot act on. Every quantity below is per account and never summed across accounts (PS-30).

A winner whose stop has trailed above its cost can no longer lose money against cost, so it releases its whole allocation without being sold. That is your mandate M-3 (sell less and let winners run) implemented in arithmetic: holding a winner frees capacity for the next idea. Marking risk from the current price instead would keep a winner charged forever and quietly penalise holding.

The gap the data leaves — F14 D4

BMO exports no open orders, so the system does not know your stops. You chose: pass --held-stop SYM=PRICE for every holding and committed risk is measured; miss any one and it is unmeasured — reported as null, never 0, with the missing names listed — and then only r_ceiling binds. A guessed stop per holding was rejected: it would invent a number you did not set.

4.3The "divide by n trades" trap — why slots are a policy

An intuitive proposal: if the remaining cash supports two more tickers, give each half the budget. The intuition (don't spend the whole budget on one trade) is right; the derivation is circular:

per-trade risk = R / n
notional       = (R / n) × (entry / d)
total over n   = n × (R / n) × (entry / d) = R × entry / d     ← n cancels

Total capital deployed is R × entry/d however you split it, so "how many trades the cash supports" cannot determine n. It has to be stated. Hence target_positions is required per-account configuration with no default, and a full account (n_reserve ≤ 0) is refused rather than sized against a floor (PS-43). Adding to a ticker you already hold sizes into that ticker's own slot (AC-F14.16).

4.4The per-trade risk — the minimum of two terms

risk_capital = min( capital × r_ceiling × E·C·P·D , (capital × r_total − committed) / n_reserve ) The second term is used only when committed risk is measured. Code: src/portfolio/sizing/trade.py::size_trade. Known answers from §5.1b, tested in AC-F14.19.
§5.1b worked

Capital 100,000 · r_ceiling 1 % · r_total 6 % · n_reserve 4.

  • Nothing committed: min(1,000, 6,000 / 4 = 1,500) = 1,000 — the ceiling binds.
  • 3,000 already committed: min(1,000, 3,000 / 4 = 750) = 750 — the reserve binds. The account is three-quarters spent on open stops, so the next trade is smaller.
  • Committed unknown: 1,000, ceiling only, and the answer says only r_ceiling binds.

PART 6

The dynamic risk budget — a feature, not a parameter

Your ruling (2026-08-18): "a fixed percentage for risk budget is meaningless in reality." This part is what the design does about it — and why most of it is still switched off.

6.1Why a fixed percentage is wrong in kind

A constant r is blind to everything that should move it: edge (a marginal setup and a strong one get the same stake), correlation (five semiconductor names at 1 % each are closer to one 4 % bet than five independent ones), drawdown state, estimation confidence, and the opportunity set (L1-portfolio-strategy §5.1d).

6.2The shape — multiplicative, bounded, decomposed

risk_budget = r_ceiling × E × C × P × D     each factor ∈ (0, 1] MethodEntry risk_budget_dynamic_v1 (src/portfolio/methods/entries/). Bounding every factor at 1 makes INV-022 structural: the product can never exceed r_ceiling, by construction rather than by a clamp bolted on afterwards.

E edgesignal strength vs the cross-sectionrank-score spread, applied only if it clears its own MDE (PS-35)unmeasured — the ranker is refused by its own gate (walk-forward IC 0.0203, t = 1.42); differentiating on noise is worse than a flat budget

A factor that cannot be measured is set to 1.0 with status unmeasured — never estimated, never quietly omitted. So today every answer reads "budget = ceiling, because E, C, P, D are not measured yet" (F14 D3). The framework degrades visibly to the fixed percentage it replaces, rather than to a smaller number that looks like caution. Each factor needs a MethodEntry with a known-answer test before it multiplies a real position (PS-36).

6.3Why not Kelly?

The Kelly criterion (Kelly, 1956) maximises long-run log growth; for one asset with expected excess return μ and variance σ², the fraction is f* = μ/σ². It produces exactly the edge × 1/vol shape wanted — and it is savage under parameter uncertainty (MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba, 2011). At IC ≈ 0.02 the error in μ dwarfs μ itself: the fundamental law (Grinold, 1989) puts the information ratio at about IC · √breadth, so a tiny IC means a tiny, noisy μ. Estimation error is the general problem — DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009) show that across fourteen estimated models none consistently beats equal weighting out of sample. A bounded product of separately measured factors, each switched on only when it clears its own error, is the design's answer.

6.4Risk parity — reported, never a target (PS-31, PS-32)

You approved reporting the equal-risk weight beside the actual one (QS-5): "equal-risk would be 6 %; you hold 31 %". It is a disclosure, the honest counterweight to a concentrated book (cf. Maillard, Roncalli & Teiletche, 2010, on equal-risk-contribution portfolios). D-06 must never rank by distance from parity, track drift toward it, or order a slate by it — that would be the target allocation PS-12 forbids, and M-2 rejects the pressure it would apply. A request for one raises TargetAllocationError.

PART 8

The policy file: config/portfolio_policy.yaml

Three numbers per account. They are yours because each one divides or caps a position, and a number that does that cannot have an invented default (PS-43).

8.1What each parameter means

KeyPlain meaningWhere it enters the codeEnforced
r_ceilingThe most one trade may lose if its stop is hit, as a fraction of the accountthe ceiling term capital × r_ceiling × E·C·P·D — today simply capital × r_ceiling0 < r_ceiling ≤ r_total

8.2How the three fit together

The ratio r_total / r_ceiling is how many full-ceiling losses the account can carry open at once. Compare it with target_positions:

  • target_positions ≈ r_total / r_ceiling — every slot can be filled at full ceiling risk, and the account then sits exactly at r_total.
  • target_positions > r_total / r_ceiling — later trades get less than the ceiling once committed risk builds up, until trailed stops release it (Part 4.2). The account self-throttles.
  • target_positions < r_total / r_ceiling — r_total never binds; the ceiling alone governs.

And mind the slot count against what is already held: TFSA_2 holds 7 lines, so target_positions of 7 or less refuses every new name (adding to a held name still works); RESP_1 holds 1.

8.4Your risk appetite — how it should and should not move these numbers

Risk appetite is the amount of loss you can bear without changing your behaviour for the worse. Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) documents that losses weigh roughly twice as heavily as equal gains, which is why the numbers should be chosen before a loss, when you are calm, and written down — which is exactly what a config file is.

r_ceiling ← pain per loss

Ask: what single stop-out would make me break my own rules next time (move a stop, average down, revenge-trade)? Set the ceiling below that. Note that M-3 flags averaging up; nothing flags the emotional response to a loss except you.

r_total ← the bad day

Under M-2 the book is deliberately concentrated in one theme, so the open positions are correlated: a sector-wide drop can hit several stops in the same week. r_total is close to "the loss on a theme crash". Choose it as that, not as a sum of independent bets.

target_positions ← concentration preference

Fewer slots means larger reserve terms per trade and a more concentrated book — consistent with M-2. More slots means smaller per-trade risk once committed risk builds. This is your breadth decision, and the code refuses to infer it from cash (Part 4.3).

Per account, not per person

The accounts are separate by law, and their purposes differ. The RESP has a beneficiary and its own mandate and is excluded from the fund (RC-4); it may deserve smaller numbers than a TFSA. In a TFSA or RRSP a realised loss also permanently uses up contribution room — there is no capital-loss offset in a registered account (PS-21: no tax event either way).

What appetite must not do
  • It must not be expressed by tightening stops to fit more shares: that raises the position, not the safety (Part 2.2), and raises the hit rate (Part 3.3).
  • A stop is not a guaranteed price. An overnight gap (earnings) can fill below it, so the realised loss can exceed r_ceiling. Choose the ceiling with that in mind; Part 10 says how to measure it.
  • Do not revise the numbers after a loss to make the last trade look acceptable. Change them deliberately, and note why.

Why F1 and F2 matter most

The whole domain rests on one claim: the stop makes the loss knowable in advance. F1 tests whether it does in dollars; F2 tests whether the volatility model knows how often it will be tested. Both need only BMO's transaction history (fills) and the sizing answers you acted on — which is an argument for keeping a note of each answer you trade on, since D-06 itself writes nothing (PS-26).

Self-check
  1. Same 1 % risk, stop 2 % vs 10 % below entry: which position is larger, and by how much?
  2. Why can't "how many trades does my cash support" decide n_reserve?
  3. A winner's stop trails from 84 to 115 with cost 90. What happens to committed risk, and which mandate rule does that serve?
  4. Why does every answer on TFSA_2 today say cash binds?
  5. What was wrong with size_for_stop, and why did its test pass?
  6. Your stop implies k = 0.46. Roughly how likely is it to be hit within a month on noise alone?
Answers
  1. The 2 % stop: five times larger (multiplier entry/d of 50 vs 10).
  2. Total deployable notional is R × entry/d for any n — n cancels (Part 4.3).
  3. It falls from 600 (per 100 shares) to 0 — the budget is released without selling (PS-29, M-3).
  4. The account holds 105.24 CAD of cash; any risk-sized position costs more.
  5. It labelled cash but returned risk-sized shares, divided the ceiling by slots, and lost the uncapped number; the test checked only the label (DX-191).
  6. About 65 % — 2(1 − Φ(0.46)) under a driftless random walk.
